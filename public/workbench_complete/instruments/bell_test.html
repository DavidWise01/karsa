// the inequality

The wall at 2

In 1964 John Bell proved something startling: if the world were local (no faster-than-light influence) and realist (measurement outcomes are predetermined), then certain correlations are mathematically capped. The CHSH form of his result says a particular combination of four correlation measurements satisfies |S| ≤ 2 — always, for any classical mechanism, hidden variables included. Quantum mechanics predicts entangled particles can reach S = 2√2. Experiments agree with quantum mechanics. The wall is real, and entanglement goes through it.

correlation for (|HH⟩+|VV⟩)/√2 : E(a,b) = cos 2(a−b) · V S = E(a,b) − E(a,b′) + E(a′,b) + E(a′,b′) classical bound |S| ≤ 2 ← local hidden variables quantum maximum |S| = 2√2 ≈ 2.828 ← Tsirelson's bound optimal angles: a=0°, a′=45°, b=22.5°, b′=67.5° visibility from fine-structure splitting: V = 1 / (1 + (FSS/Γ)²) S crosses below 2 once V drops under 1/√2 ≈ 0.707

The simulation draws real random outcomes weighted by quantum probabilities, so the displayed S fluctuates and converges with more pairs — exactly like a real coincidence-counting experiment accumulating statistics.

// reading the bench

What the numbers say

S > 2 (green)

The classical wall is broken. No local-realist theory can produce this data. The dot's photons are genuinely entangled — proof, not assertion.

S → 2.83

At optimal angles with perfect visibility you approach Tsirelson's bound, the maximum quantum mechanics itself allows. The ribbon, quantified.

FSS opens → S falls

Raise the fine-structure splitting and which-path information leaks in; visibility drops, S sinks back below 2, and the proof evaporates. Same physics as the previous bench, now on a meter.

// why anyone cares

The most consequential inequality

  • It settled the Einstein–Bohr debate empirically. Bell turned a philosophical argument about whether quantum mechanics is "complete" into a measurable number. Nature chose quantum.
  • 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics. Aspect, Clauser, and Zeilinger won for experimental Bell tests and entanglement — the experiment this bench simulates.
  • Device-independent security. A Bell violation certifies entanglement without trusting the hardware — the foundation of the strongest quantum-cryptography protocols.
  • The dot as a certified source. Showing S > 2 from quantum-dot photons (Stevenson, Akopian, 2006) proved a semiconductor can emit genuinely entangled light — the whole point of the previous tool, now verified.
// honesty about the model

Where this simplifies

  • Loophole-free is hard. Real landmark tests had to close detection and locality loopholes with fast switching and distant detectors; this simulation assumes ideal detection.
  • Visibility model is schematic. The V = 1/(1+(FSS/Γ)²) washout captures the right trend (which-path → decoherence) but real fidelity also depends on timing, spin scattering, and re-excitation.
  • Perfect analyzers. Ideal polarizers and unit detector efficiency; real optics add loss and misalignment that lower the measured S.
  • Statistics are simulated. Counts are Monte-Carlo draws from the quantum probabilities, illustrating convergence — not data from a physical dot.

Background: J. S. Bell (1964); Clauser–Horne–Shimony–Holt (CHSH, 1969); Aspect et al. (1982); Nobel Prize in Physics 2022 (Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger). Quantum-dot entangled-pair Bell tests: Stevenson et al., Akopian et al. (2006).
