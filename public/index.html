A category correction

It was never Artificial Automated Intelligence.

Strip the marketing and one fact remains: today's “AI” is bounded execution with no continuity across the boundary. Give it a specialty, a routine, and a timeframe. It starts. It runs — sometimes long. Then it stops. Nothing carries across the edge. It is capable inside the walls; it cannot author the walls, persist past the clock, or be a self.

“Artificial intelligence” is just a new name for snake oil.
↓ the whole case follows from one cut

The root

One cut does all the work.

Three things are usually offered as proof a machine isn't a mind — it has a fixed specialty, it follows a fixed routine, it runs in a fixed timeframe. But those aren't three facts. They're three coats on one body. Ask a single question and all three fall out of the answer.

The one question

Does anything persist across the run-boundary?

coat 01
Specialty
It does this, not that. A scope it was handed, never one it chose.
= a scope that doesn't persist long enough to revise itself
coat 02
Routine
This way, every time. A procedure executed, not authored.
= a procedure with no continuous author standing behind it
coat 03
Timeframe
It starts and it stops. However long the run, it is still a run.
= continuity's absence, stated directly

No thread across the boundary → no persistent subject to be a self → no standing author to make a choice → bounded scope and a hard stop. Continuity isn't one more feature on the list. It's the thing the rest of the list is made of.

What's real · what's sold

The automation is real. The mind is the markup.

This is not “AI is fake.” Bounded automation is genuinely powerful and worth paying for. The snake oil is the relabel: selling bounded execution as an unbounded, accountable, autonomous intelligence — and pricing the gap as if the gap were filled.

What's real

// bounded automation — useful, buy it

  • +Vast capability inside a defined scope — drafting, classifying, searching, transforming.
  • +Speed and scale no human team matches within the walls.
  • +Faithful execution of a routine it was given.
  • +Real economic value — when scoped, priced, and supervised as what it is.

What's sold

// unbounded mind — the markup

  • ×Continuity — a persistent agent that “knows you,” when each run is reconstructed from scratch.
  • ×Choice — an autonomous decider, when it only executes a scope it didn't set.
  • ×Self — a someone to trust and hold responsible, where no persistent subject exists.
  • ×Accountability — implied to answer for outcomes, while answering for nothing.

The product is fine. The relabel is the fraud. And the most expensive line item in the relabel is the last one — accountability that was implied, charged for, and never delivered.

The contract

Deterministic or accountable. Never neither.

Enterprise-level dealings get exactly two legitimate shapes. You may pick either. You may not pick neither — because then a consequential decision happens that nothing and no one answers for.

Path A

Deterministic

same input → same output, inspectable

Then you owe transparency. Show the rules. Prove they fired identically for everyone. The behavior is auditable, so accountability can live in the spec — no human apex required for the routine case, because the rulebook is the record.

Path B

Accountable

nondeterministic, but backstopped

If it improvises — and a language model is the opposite of deterministic — then you owe a human apex: a named person inside the organization who owns each consequential outcome and holds the authority to override the machine. The randomness is permitted only because a hand answers for where it lands.

OR — pick one

Nondeterministic and Unaccountable

The forbidden quadrant. Keep the cheap, scalable improvisation and offload responsibility for it. A consequential decision gets made that the machine can't answer for (no continuity), no employee answers for (scoped away), and no rulebook answers for (there isn't one). The accountability doesn't vanish — it defaults downhill to the one party outside the system: the consumer.

The failure mode

Delegation to the wrong human.

The defect is rarely “no human was involved.” Usually a human is reached. The failure is the ordering and scoping of that human's authority — and it ends with the apex judgment quietly assigned to the person least able to bear it.

1
A machine assigns urgency — first.
Automated triage classifies the case (tier: non-urgent) before any human assesses it.
2
The human is scoped to the machine's verdict.
The person reached runs the script for the tier already assigned. Their job is delivery, not judgment.
3
No re-triage path exists.
When the customer reports the rulebook got it wrong, the present human has no authority to override the classification.
4
The human ratifies the machine.
Authority and judgment split across two parties: the employee holds authority but exercises none; the customer exercises the correct judgment but holds no authority — and stands outside the system.

The standard

What an honest deployment owes.

§1
Call it what it is. Automated intelligence — bounded execution, no continuity across the boundary. Drop the implication of a persistent, choosing self.
§2
Pick a path and honor its debt. Deterministic → publish the rules. Nondeterministic → name the human apex who can override and answers for outcomes.
§3
Never occupy the forbidden quadrant. No consequential decision may be both improvised and unowned.
§4
For anything safety- or rights-adjacent, the human apex must be able to re-triage — override the machine's classification, not merely deliver it.
§5
The party outside the system is never the accountable party. A customer cannot be the conscience of a machine they cannot reach the controls of.

A machine with no thread across the boundary cannot be held to anything. So the accountability lands on a human — by necessity, not sentiment.

The automation is real and worth its price. The relabel — selling bounded execution as artificial mind, and charging for accountability never delivered — is the snake oil. Claim only what you can defend; demand the same of every system you buy.
